Un asistente de IA puede darte un feedback muy detallado sobre lo que escribes en inglés, pero solo si le das la información que necesita. Si le pegas el texto y le pides “corrígemelo”, te devolverá algo genérico. Si sigues estos tres pasos, te dará una corrección, una lista de errores y una nota para cada criterio.

Paso 1: la tarea

Copia el enunciado de tu tarea en el chat. Si no tienes una, aquí tienes un ejemplo para cada nivel.

Ejemplo de tarea · A2
Your English friend Sam is coming to visit your town next month. Write an email to Sam. Tell Sam what the weather is usually like at that time of year, one place you want to show them, and one thing you can do together. Write 60 to 80 words.
Ejemplo de tarea · B1
Write a story for your English class blog. Your story must end with this sentence: "And that was the day I decided to try again." Write 120 to 160 words.
Ejemplo de tarea · B2
Your town's English-language website is asking readers for their opinions. Write an article answering this question: Should everyone learn to cook at school? Give your reasons and include an example from your own experience. Write 150 to 200 words.
Ejemplo de tarea · C1
Your company wants to reduce stress among its staff. Write a proposal for your manager suggesting two changes, explaining the benefits of each one and recommending which should come first. Write 230 to 270 words.
Ejemplo de tarea · C2
Write an essay for an online magazine discussing whether technology has made us better or worse at paying attention. Look at the question from different points of view and reach a clear conclusion of your own. Write 300 to 350 words.

Paso 2: los criterios de tu nivel

Busca tu nivel y copia el bloque entero en el chat, debajo de la tarea. Los he escrito yo para que la IA te dé un feedback útil y concreto, así que no son los de ningún examen oficial, aunque miran lo mismo que mira cualquier corrector de writing. Son cuatro: Task (¿haces todo lo que te piden?), Reader (¿el texto funciona para quien lo lee?), Flow (¿se sigue bien de principio a fin?) y Language (¿cómo usas el vocabulario y la gramática?). Lo que cambia de un nivel a otro es lo que se pide en cada uno.

Criterios A2

Copia y pega en el chat
How to mark this A2 text
Score each criterion from 1 to 5. The descriptions for 5, 3 and 1 are a guide: give 4 or 2 when the text sits between two descriptions.

Task: Did the writer do everything the task asked?
5: Every point in the task is covered, so the reader gets all the information they need.
3: Most points are covered, but one is missing or answered in just a few words.
1: Several points are missing, so the reader would have to write back and ask.

Reader: Would the reader understand the message easily, and does it look like the right kind of text?
5: The message is easy to understand on the first read and looks like a real email or message, with a greeting and a sign-off.
3: The message is mostly clear, but the reader has to read one or two sentences again.
1: The reader has to guess what several sentences mean.

Flow: Does the text follow a clear order?
5: The ideas follow a clear order, and simple words like and, but, because and so join them correctly.
3: There is some order, but some sentences are just listed one after another without being joined.
1: The ideas jump around and aren't joined, so it reads like a list of separate sentences.

Language: Are the words and the grammar right for this level?
5: Everyday words are used correctly, and the present simple, past simple and basic questions are mostly correct.
3: There are several mistakes with everyday words or basic grammar, but the meaning is still clear.
1: Mistakes are so frequent that some sentences can't be understood.

Criterios B1

Copia y pega en el chat
How to mark this B1 text
Score each criterion from 1 to 5. The descriptions for 5, 3 and 1 are a guide: give 4 or 2 when the text sits between two descriptions.

Task: Did the writer answer every part of the task?
5: Every part of the task is answered, each with a little detail or an example.
3: Every part is mentioned, but one or two get a single short sentence or drift off the topic.
1: Part of the task is missing or has been misunderstood.

Reader: Does it sound like the kind of text it's meant to be?
5: It sounds right for its type: a friendly email sounds friendly, and a story makes the reader want to know what happens next.
3: The type of text is right, but the tone slips in places, for example too formal for a friend or too flat for a story.
1: It doesn't read like the type of text that was asked for.

Flow: Is it easy to follow from start to finish?
5: Each paragraph has a clear purpose, and linking words like first, then, however, also and in the end guide the reader.
3: There are paragraphs, but there are few linking words or the same ones keep coming back.
1: It's one long block, or a list of sentences joined only with and or but.

Language: How varied and accurate are the vocabulary and grammar?
5: Good everyday vocabulary with some variety, and past tenses, the present perfect and future forms are used with few mistakes.
3: The vocabulary is fine but repetitive, and longer sentences have mistakes, although the meaning stays clear.
1: Only simple words and short sentences, with mistakes in basic grammar.

Criterios B2

Copia y pega en el chat
How to mark this B2 text
Score each criterion from 1 to 5. The descriptions for 5, 3 and 1 are a guide: give 4 or 2 when the text sits between two descriptions.

Task: Is every point developed, and is everything on topic?
5: Every point is developed with reasons or examples, and nothing is off topic.
3: All the points are there, but one is underdeveloped or a sentence or two wander away from the task.
1: A point is missing, or the writer has misunderstood part of the task.

Reader: Does the text do its job for the person reading it?
5: The level of formality fits the reader, and the text does what its type should do: an essay persuades, a review helps someone decide, an article keeps people reading. Opinions are explained, not just stated.
3: The formality is mostly right and the main purpose is achieved, but the text is a little plain and doesn't really draw the reader in.
1: The formality is wrong in many places, or it's not clear what the text is trying to do.

Flow: Are the ideas well organised and connected?
5: Each paragraph has one clear main idea, and the ideas are connected in varied ways, with linking words (although, whereas, as a result, on the other hand) and with words like this, which and such that avoid repeating the same nouns.
3: Clear paragraphs and a decent range of linking words, although some are repeated or feel a little forced.
1: The paragraphs are unclear and the ideas are joined with only a handful of basic linking words.

Language: Does the writer show range as well as accuracy?
5: There is real range: some less everyday words and natural word combinations, plus a mix of structures (relative clauses, passives, conditionals) used well. Small slips don't get in the way.
3: Mostly everyday vocabulary with a few attempts at more precise words, and some longer structures with mistakes that don't block the meaning.
1: Simple vocabulary that keeps repeating, mostly simple sentences, and mistakes the reader notices.

Criterios C1

Copia y pega en el chat
How to mark this C1 text
Score each criterion from 1 to 5. The descriptions for 5, 3 and 1 are a guide: give 4 or 2 when the text sits between two descriptions.

Task: Are the ideas explored in depth?
5: Every point is explored in depth and evaluated, not just described, and the conclusion follows from what came before.
3: The points are relevant and mostly well developed, but one is thinner than the rest or the conclusion is predictable.
1: Ideas are described rather than evaluated, or part of the task is thin or off topic.

Reader: Is the text type handled with confidence, and are complex ideas easy to follow?
5: The writer handles the text type with confidence (headings in a report, a clear recommendation in a proposal, a balanced argument in an essay), and complex ideas are easy to follow.
3: The text type is right and the ideas are clear, although complex points sometimes need a second read.
1: Key features of the text type are missing, or the complex ideas get lost.

Flow: Does it read smoothly from start to finish?
5: It reads smoothly from start to finish. Ideas are linked naturally in many different ways, including signposting phrases (what's more, that said, it follows that), and no transition feels forced.
3: Well organised and clear throughout, with varied linking that is sometimes a little formulaic.
1: Organised, but the linking is limited or mechanical, for example Firstly, Secondly and Finally at the start of every paragraph.

Language: How precise and natural is the language?
5: Precise, natural vocabulary, including idiomatic phrases and accurate word combinations, and advanced grammar (inversion, cleft sentences, participle clauses) used naturally with very few errors.
3: A good range with some less common vocabulary, and advanced structures used well with occasional errors.
1: A reasonable range, but the word choice isn't always precise and advanced structures often go wrong.

Criterios C2

Copia y pega en el chat
How to mark this C2 text
Score each criterion from 1 to 5. The descriptions for 5, 3 and 1 are a guide: give 4 or 2 when the text sits between two descriptions.

Task: How thorough and nuanced is the content?
5: The content is thorough and nuanced: the writer looks at different sides, qualifies their claims and leaves the reader with a full picture.
3: Thorough and relevant, but some points are stated rather than explored.
1: Relevant but superficial, with gaps or claims that aren't precise.

Reader: How persuasive and controlled is the writing?
5: Persuasive and elegant. The writer controls tone precisely (emphasis, understatement, a touch of irony where it fits), so even abstract ideas land with no effort from the reader.
3: Confident and clear, with the right tone, but without much personal style.
1: Appropriate, but flat or hesitant when the ideas get abstract.

Flow: Does the structure carry the argument?
5: The structure feels inevitable: the argument builds step by step, and the connections are made so naturally that the reader hardly notices them.
3: Clearly structured and connected throughout, with varied linking.
1: Generally well organised, but some linking feels repetitive or mechanical.

Language: Is the language at the level of a skilled writer?
5: Wide, precise and idiomatic vocabulary, used the way a skilled native writer would use it, and grammar fully under control.
3: Sophisticated vocabulary used precisely and strong control of grammar, with a few small slips.
1: A good range, but precision and natural idiomatic use aren't always there.
